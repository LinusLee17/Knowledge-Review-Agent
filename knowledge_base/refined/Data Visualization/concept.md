## Definition
Data visualization uses graphical representations to reveal patterns, comparisons, trends, and uncertainty in data. A good visualization matches the analytical question and the audience's decision needs.

## Why it Matters
Data Visualization turns observations into evidence for Decision Support, Exploratory Analysis, Reporting, helping decide what to investigate or model next.

## Core Intuition
Choose visual encodings that make the important comparison or pattern easy to see before adding decoration.

## How it Works
1. Clarify the question, audience, and decision.
2. Select data and visual encodings that fit the comparison or pattern.
3. Remove misleading clutter, test readability, and communicate the result with appropriate context.

## Key Components
- Analytical question
- Data and transformations
- Visual encodings
- Audience and context
- Readability and integrity checks

## Common Mistakes
- Choosing a chart before clarifying the question and audience.
- Using truncated axes, misleading scales, or decorative encodings.
- Showing every available field instead of emphasizing the decision-relevant signal.

## Real-world Applications
- Decision Support
