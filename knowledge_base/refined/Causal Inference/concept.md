## Definition
Causal Inference estimates the effect of an intervention or treatment, separating causal effects from association. It requires assumptions or designs that identify the counterfactual comparison.

## Why it Matters
Causal Inference provides a formal way to support Business Experiments, Policy Evaluation, Treatment Effects under stated assumptions.

## Core Intuition
Compare what happened with what would have happened under a different treatment or intervention.

## How it Works
Prerequisites: Experiment Design, Probability.
1. Specify inputs and objective.
2. Apply method steps under stated assumptions.
3. Produce output, estimate, or decision.
4. Evaluate output against an explicit criterion.
Typical use context: Business Experiments, Policy Evaluation, Treatment Effects.
Compare against: Bayesian Networks, Experiment Design, Internal Validity.

## Key Components
- Prerequisites: Experiment Design, Probability
- Assumptions: Assumptions for causal inference with Fixed Effects Regression, Can obtain causal effect using observational data if assumptions are met, Entity Fixed Effects regression – assumptions for causal inference, Least Squares Assumptions for causal inference
