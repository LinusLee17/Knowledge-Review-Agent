## Definition
Bayesian Optimization is sequential black-box optimization for expensive evaluations. It fits a probabilistic surrogate to observed configurations and costs, then uses an acquisition function to choose the next configuration, balancing exploration and exploitation.

## Why it Matters
BO is useful when one trial is expensive and the objective is a black box. It uses completed trials to avoid spending the next evaluation in clearly unpromising regions.

## Core Intuition
Use a model of what is known about the objective to decide which unknown experiment is worth running next.

## How it Works
1. Evaluate an initial design of configurations and store observations in a trial set.
2. Fit or update a surrogate model of validation cost, including uncertainty when available.
3. Optimize an acquisition function such as expected improvement or probability of improvement.
4. Evaluate the selected configuration, append the result, and repeat until the evaluation budget is exhausted.

## Mathematical Foundation
$$
x_t = \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} a(x; \mathcal{D}_{t-1})
$$

The surrogate models the unknown objective from observations `\mathcal{D}_{t-1}`; acquisition function `a` selects the next expensive evaluation.

## Key Components
- Search space
- Observed trials
- Surrogate model
- Predictive uncertainty
- Acquisition function
- Evaluation budget

## Advantages
- Uses prior observations, so it can be efficient when evaluations are expensive.
- Supports noisy objectives and mixed search spaces through suitable surrogates.
- Acquisition functions expose the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Limitations
- Surrogate and acquisition quality can degrade in very high-dimensional or highly conditional spaces.
- Sequential decisions limit parallel throughput unless batch methods are used.
- A misleading surrogate or noisy validation signal can direct trials to the wrong region.

## Common Mistakes
- Using Bayesian optimization when evaluations are cheap or dimension is too high for a useful surrogate.
- Ignoring noisy validation scores when fitting the surrogate and acquisition function.
- Confusing acquisition optimization with the expensive model evaluation it selects.
